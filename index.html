The Keeper Was Not Tending the Light

On December 30, 2023, America's last lighthouse keeper retired from a light that had been automatic for twenty-five years. On October 1, 2026, Britain told its ships to practise finding their position by looking at the coast again.

OCTOBER 15, 2026 · 7 min read

On December 30, 2023, Sally Snowman retired. She had been keeper of Boston Light for twenty years, the seventieth keeper in the station's history and the first woman to hold the post. The light stands on Little Brewster Island in Boston Harbor, where she had first arrived as a ten-year-old on a picnic with her father. She married there in 1994. She ended her watch as the last official lighthouse keeper in the United States.

The detail that rearranges the rest: Boston Light was automated in 1998. For all twenty years of her tenure, and for five years before she arrived, the light turned itself on, rotated itself, and flashed every ten seconds without a keeper's hand anywhere near it. It still does, out to twenty-seven nautical miles.
