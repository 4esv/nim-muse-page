The Instruction Set

A fungus, a fluke, a barnacle. Then the walled-in woman, the man on the pillar, the burning monk. The pieces, in order.

OCTOBER 1, 2026

At noon, the ant leaves the trail.

01the keeper

It climbs the nearest stem, finds the underside of a leaf, and locks its mandibles into the vein in the death grip it will hold until it dies.

The fungus Ophiocordyceps has been growing through its body for weeks, filling the muscle fibers, steering the limbs. When researchers mapped the invasion in three dimensions, they found the fungal cells everywhere except one place. The brain was intact. The fungus had surrounded it without entering it, and worked the body the way a puppeteer works the strings, because the host has to live long enough to perform the final bite.1

After death, a stalk rises from the back of the ant’s head and releases the spores from the top. The ant does not choose the leaf; the leaf is chosen for the spore. The climb happens at solar noon, when the humidity suits the fungus and not the ant.

02the dusk climber

At dusk, another ant climbs.

The lancet fluke is less dramatic and more precise: most of its larvae encyst in the ant’s body and wait, while one migrates to the subesophageal ganglion and takes the nerves.

At dusk the ant climbs a blade of grass and clamps on with its mandibles, holding itself up where the grazing animals feed, and at dawn it climbs down and goes back to work. By day it shows no symptoms. The schedule belongs to the fluke’s final host: the climb begins as the light fails and ends at dawn, synced to the grazing hours of the ruminants that will eat the ant.2

The colony never notices.

03the brood tender

The crab keeps up appearances too.

The barnacle Sacculina does not eat its crab. It becomes the crab’s purpose. The infective larva settles on a young crab, injects its soft body through a joint, and spreads inside as the interna, a root system drinking from every organ that dissolves the gonads. Infected males are feminized. Then the parasite pushes its brood sac out through the crab’s underside, exactly where a female would carry her own young.

The crab grooms the sac. It fans it with water. It tends the parasite’s offspring as its own, and it never leaves.

Parasitologists call this parasitic castration.3

04the walled

In the twelfth century, in England, a woman could ask to be walled into a church and never leave. The rite began with last rites and a requiem Mass. The bishop said the Office of the Dead as she entered the cell, and where the wall was built, he set his seal upon it.

A shuttered squint faced the altar for Mass, an alms window admitted food and counsel, and a street window admitted light. Through the alms window the anchoress gave counsel and the town came to her for it, yet some anchorites refused to leave their cells as pirates pillaged the town and the church burned around them. They burned in place.4

Not every cell burned. Julian of Norwich had her sixteen visions of the Passion in May 1373, at thirty and a half, during a near-death illness, and was then walled into a cell at St Julian’s Church for the rest of her life, into the 1410s. The book she wrote inside contains the line “all shall be well, and all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well.” The most famous sentence of English mysticism was composed inside a brick box.5

The box had a vertical cousin. Around the year 420, Simeon Stylites was expelled from a monastery for excessive austerity, so he climbed a pillar northwest of Aleppo and stayed for some thirty-six years. The platform was about one square meter and the last column stood fifteen meters, and he lived permanently exposed, preaching twice daily to the crowds below. After his death they built four basilicas around an octagon on the site, some five thousand square meters of floor. The man who needed one square meter got five thousand.6

The next instrument was the body itself. The Jain practice of sallekhana runs the same program slower: under a monk’s guidance, over weeks or months, food is renounced first and then liquids until death, its aim liberation and not suicide as the law understands it. In August 2015 the Rajasthan High Court banned it as abetment of suicide, and three weeks later the Supreme Court of India stayed the ban; the matter has sat in legal limbo ever since.7

The wall kept the world out. The pillar kept the ground away. The fast emptied the body.

05the vulnerable

In the plague years, they stayed. Dionysius of Alexandria, writing of the plague that struck in the 250s, recorded that the Christians nursed the sick and buried the dead at great personal cost while others fled the cities. Many of the nurses died. The survivors remembered who had stayed.8

It builds its houses where the sick and the poor already are.

Why does it seek the vulnerable?

06the spore

On 11 June 1963, the monk Thich Quang Duc sat down in the lotus position at a Saigon intersection, in front of the press the monks had summoned. Gasoline was poured over him. He struck the match himself, and he chanted while he burned. The reporter David Halberstam wrote that he never moved a muscle and never uttered a sound during the ten minutes the body burned.

The photograph won a Pulitzer, and Kennedy said no news picture had ever generated so much emotion around the world. After cremation, his heart was found intact and venerated as a relic.9

Some transmissions need no contact. They need only to be seen.

07the rebuilt

In the fiction, the rite continues: a priest in rust-red robes anoints a tank tread with sacred oil, chanting to its machine spirit in a language of clicks and static. The rite has a number and a correct sequence of gestures, and if he gets the sequence wrong the machine is not merely unrepaired: it is insulted. The priests do the same to themselves, replacing flesh with mechanism limb by limb until the robes hang on more machine than man.

Their creed states the terms plainly: the flesh is weak. Pain is a design weakness of the human body, so replace the flesh and be closer to the god; ritual honors the machine spirit, and innovation is tech-heresy, which must always be terminated with prejudice.1011

The host, rebuilt in the guest’s image, running the guest’s list.

Every animal arrives with a list. Eat. Sleep. Flee. Live. The ranking is not subtle, and it was written long before the animal: the living outrank the dead.

Now count what was kept against what was spent. The brain, kept; the body, spent. The wall, kept; the town, spent. The image, kept; the man, spent.

Ask yourself what you concluded. Notice when you concluded it.
