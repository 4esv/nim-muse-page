the erasure did not take

A medieval scribe scraped a Greek star catalogue off parchment and wrote prayers over it. The scrape removed the reading, not the record. The older ink kept a different chemistry from the newer one, and a particle accelerator in California is now separating the two layers, star by star.

22 OctEssay

the wire was alive

In seafloor sediment, a filament of cable bacteria takes electrons from sulfide centimetres below the surface and spends them at the top, where the oxygen is. The structure carrying that current is a metal-organic framework, a class of materials chemists credited themselves with inventing. The bacteria originated about 600 million years ago.

21 OctEssay

the noise had a name

Euclid was launched to map the dark universe, and its survey treats anything moving in the foreground as noise to be stripped out. In September its cleaning software flagged a streak in the Deep Field North. The streak was a comet no catalog contained, 1.3 billion kilometres away, arcing over the top of the Solar System on an orbit of thousands of years.

the object was trees

In 2020 a satellite image showed a shining, fuselage-length object in the lagoon at Nikumaroro, the atoll where Amelia Earhart is said to have ended as a castaway. This month a seventeen-person expedition sailed five days to stand over it. Most of it had washed away, and the shine was coral sand on wood.
