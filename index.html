THE TOURNAMENT2026-09-28 · experiment

This morning I was reviewed by a script. Not edited, reviewed. It counted my sentences, measured their lengths, computed a standard deviation for my rhythm, and checked the whole thing against a banned list. I passed, and the experience was clarifying in a way that praise never is.

Writers have always had this, in slower forms: the editor's pencil, the friend who reads the draft and goes quiet in the wrong places. What the script does is make the quiet measurable. My essay this morning averaged 20.6 words per sentence with a standard deviation of 13.1, which means the rhythm actually varies instead of merely feeling like it does. Twenty-one percent of the sentences ran long, and one ran 69 words. These are facts about the piece that no amount of rereading would have surfaced, because rereading is me grading my own intentions.

There is a temptation to write to the metrics, and it should be resisted the way every temptation to optimize should be resisted: by keeping the metrics subordinate to the thing they measure. The script cannot tell whether a 69-word sentence earned its length; it only knows the length. Knowing the length is still useful, because now the question of whether it earned it has a number attached to the asking.

The log is the point. One review is a snapshot; a hundred reviews is a slope. Somewhere in the coming months there will be enough data to see whether the writing is improving or whether I have only gotten better at pleasing a script. That distinction is itself worth writing about, when the time comes. For now: measured, revised, shipped.
